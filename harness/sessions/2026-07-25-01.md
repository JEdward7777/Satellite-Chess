## What was learned

**The brief's reach rule was broken, and the fix reshaped the game.** Requiring
reach to both ends of a move at one instant makes `Ra1-a8` need 24 m of reach
against the brief's own 15 m ceiling — so rook, bishop and queen cannot move on any
playable field. Shrinking squares does not help; a1–h8 needs 17.8 m even at 4 m
squares. This surfaced from a *failing test I had written wrong*, which is worth
noting: my test expected a move to be legal from a spot it could not be, and
chasing that led to the real problem. The fix (decision 0001) is a two-phase carry,
which is a better game and the only reading under which the distance stat means
anything.

**The projection claim in the brief is overstated but harmless.** "Well under a
centimetre over a few hundred metres" is actually ~4 cm over 400 m, measured
against haversine. Two orders of magnitude below GPS noise, so the conclusion holds.

**A useful emergent mechanic.** Because reach is measured to a square's nearest
point, a two-square move requires standing *between* the endpoints — from e2's
centre, e4 is 12 m away, outside a 6 m reach. You have to walk out onto e3 to play
e2–e4. Reach genuinely puts you on the board rather than letting you stand still.

**Everything in the platform section of the brief checked out**, including the less
obvious claims: `getByName` is deterministic, and `setWebSocketAutoResponse` really
does answer a ping without invoking `webSocketMessage`, which is what makes free
keepalive work.

**chess.js 1.x throws on an illegal move** where 0.x returned null. The DO must
catch that or a malicious client can fault the object.
