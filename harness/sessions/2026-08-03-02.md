## What was learned

**The joiner keeps the field, and the reason it can be silent is consent, not
convenience** (decision 0027). A link is a *message* — it can be opened out of
curiosity or forwarded three times — so it is always asked about. A seat is an
*act*: deliberate, recorded at the far end, and immediately followed by an hour of
walking around the field in question. There is no reading of "I joined a game
here" that does not include "I know where here is". The runner-up was asking on
the way out, once the game ends; it was rejected because it only fires for games
that finish cleanly on the phone that is still looking, and a game suspended for a
month (decision 0025) never reaches it.

**Provenance is what makes copying survivable, and the key must be inherited
rather than re-derived.** Without a lineage key, a weekly fixture on the same
common leaves fifty-two identical fields and a friend's re-calibration arrives as
a stranger. With a key derived fresh at each hop, A → B → C stops matching after
one forward. It is an eight-byte digest of the original field id — only equality
is ever asked of it, and the full UUID would have cost forty-eight base64
characters of QR for a value nobody reads.

**h8 is an offset in metres, not in degrees, and that is not a micro-optimisation.**
A degree of longitude is a different distance at every latitude, so quantising
degrees puts a square-size error into the format that varies with where on the
planet you are. Decimetres of east and north quantise the thing that matters,
uniformly: 0.1 m on the diagonal is about 0.01 m on a square edge, a hundredth of
the GPS error the game is built to absorb (decision 0023).

**Decision 0016 promised something the app could not do.** "They own it outright:
they can rename it, re-calibrate it, or delete it" — and none of those three was
reachable from anywhere in the app. There was no field screen at all; tapping a
field opened the board. That is why 6.4 grew a screen rather than just a button:
a field that arrives on a phone with no way to be rid of it is not a gift, and
decision 0027 could not have been taken without one.

**A truncated field link decodes into a perfectly good field, which nearly made a
useless test.** The name is at the end of the blob, so cutting the last twelve
characters off a link removes part of the *name* and leaves the geometry intact —
the driver's "a link that got cut in half" check would have passed against a
successful decode. It now cuts to twenty characters of blob, short enough to be
missing its geometry.

**O-11 is closed, and the old hypothesis was wrong in a way worth recording.** It
reproduced on the first full-suite run of the session. The failing test was
`carry.test.ts > credits a disconnect to whoever went missing`, asserting
`suspended_by === 'b'` and getting `null`. O-11 guessed at a `Client.next()`
wall-clock timeout under load — but that test does not wait on the clock at all,
it drives the alarm itself, so acting on the guess would have meant scaling a
timeout the failing test never uses. The real cause is a write race: `black.close()`
returns immediately, the test writes a `disconnect` timer due in the past, and the
DO's own `onDisconnect` then `INSERT OR REPLACE`s it with one `DISCONNECT_GRACE_MS`
in the future. The alarm finds nothing due. Fixed by waiting for white's next
`state` broadcast, which `onDisconnect` sends after it writes. **Three of the four
flakes this suite has produced have been this shape** (O-07, O-09, O-11): a test
writing to a Durable Object's storage without waiting for that object's own
handlers. `close()` is not a synchronisation point.

**A field name in the join-code type face reads as a serial number.** The offer
screen reused `.invite-code`, which is tracked out and set in the code face
because six characters get read aloud across a field. "The common" in that
treatment looked like a part number. One `.field-name` modifier; found by looking
at the screenshot, as every view problem in this project has been.

## Half-finished

Nothing.

Two things deliberately not done, both out of scope rather than forgotten:

- **The field screen has no map or board preview.** "Open the board" is one tap
  away and draws the real thing, so a second rendering of the same geometry on
  the screen above it would be work for its own sake.
- **`origin.version` is a lineage counter with no conflict handling.** If two
  people who both hold a copy each re-calibrate, both branches sit at the same
  version and neither is offered as an update to the other. The cost is that an
  improvement is not offered; nothing is lost or overwritten. A real fix needs
  ordering that a self-contained twelve-byte link cannot carry, and the situation
  needs two people to independently re-tap the same ground.
