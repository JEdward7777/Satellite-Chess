## What was done

Phase 3 of a four-phase pipeline run (`harness/pipeline/ratchet.md`): O-33
(done in `2026-09-24-02`), O-31 (done in `2026-09-24-03`), then **O-30**, then
**pinch zoom**. One commit carries this phase. New decision **0045**, which
supersedes 0011 (the old "Unicode glyphs, not artwork" decision).

The owner's words set the direction: the main problem was "people not knowing
which piece is theirs", and "this should be a solved problem". It is, so the
default is now what every board app does.

- **The standard two-sided set (`10.7.1`).** Colin M. L. Burnett's "cburnett"
  set, the one lichess and Wikipedia use: white pieces white with black line
  work, black pieces black with white interior lines. The twelve Commons SVGs
  are transcribed into `client/pieces.ts` as path data and drawn with cached
  `Path2D` objects. They are bundled, never fetched, so they work offline.
  License: each Commons page offers GFDL, BSD and GPL; we use **BSD**
  (3-clause). The notice is in a new `NOTICE`, in a `/*!` comment that
  survives minification, and under "Board" on the account screen. The bundle
  grew 5.5 kB gzipped (47 → 52 kB).
- **Mid-tone squares.** Light `#e6dcbc`, dark `#769656` (chess.com's green).
  A black piece on a dark square goes from 4.2:1 to 6.3:1. A white piece on a
  light square is separated by its black outline, which is what the outline
  is for. The outline is never the opponent's color any more, which was O-30.
- **Every surface uses the same art.** `pieceSvg` builds inline SVG from the
  same layers for the carry readout and the promotion picker, on a small
  light-square tile. The review screen draws no board.
- **The owner's alternative, a team disc (`10.7.2`).** A disc of the team's
  color under each piece, with a gray ring (`#8a8a8a`) so a white disc shows on
  a light square. On its black disc, a black piece's outline turns the same
  gray; black on black hid the pawn completely. The switch is per phone, in
  `localStorage` inside try/catch (`client/piece-look.ts`), and is on the
  account screen, the game screen's readout ("Pieces"), and the simulator
  panel. The default is Standard.
- **Last-move highlight (`10.7.3`).** Both squares of the last completed move
  get a translucent yellow tint, taken from the snapshot's `lastMove`. No new
  traffic. It is a fill where the square under foot and the lifted-from marker
  are outlines, and it sits under the blue reach tint so reach always reads the
  same. A predicted place moves it at the tap: `predict` fills a `lastMove`
  whose `san` is `''` and `carriedM` is 0, which nothing on the client reads.
- **Draw order.** Coordinates and destination dots now draw over the pieces
  (the bigger art hid them, including the dot on a capture square). Labels are
  sized from the narrow way across a cell (`pieceBoxPx`), sit just inside its
  corners in board-index space, and carry a thin outline in the opposite tone.
  The north arrow draws under the pieces. Pieces fit the narrow way across a
  cell, so they no longer spill on rectangular fields.
- **Tests:** piece-look storage (including a throwing store), inks and SVG for
  all twelve pieces in both looks, `pieceBoxPx`, the predicted `lastMove`, and
  the carry readout without a glyph. 1035 → **1052**.
- **Drivers**, on `wrangler dev` :8851 from a fresh `--persist-to` each time:
  `drive-game` passed (three times, once modified to switch looks mid-game and
  reload), and `check-review` passed twice. A standalone renderer harness in
  the scratchpad photographed both looks at phone size, at small cells and
  dpr 1, on a 3:1 and a 5:1 field, and from both seats.

## What was learned

- **Bigger art changes the draw order.** Anything that used to sit beside a
  small glyph (labels, destination dots, the north arrow) was suddenly under or
  over a piece that fills the cell. Look at every overlay again when a piece
  grows.
- **The mean cell is the wrong size for anything drawn inside a cell** on a
  rectangular field. `pieceBoxPx` is the narrow way across; use it.
- **A black outline is no outline on a black disc.** The disc look needed its
  own ink, which is why the art names inks (body, line, detail) rather than
  colors.
