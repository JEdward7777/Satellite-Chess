## What was learned

1. **The cookie was never renewed.** Sliding renewal (2.2.2) slid the KV record,
   but the cookie was set once, at sign-in, with a 30-day `Max-Age`. So the
   browser dropped a daily player's perfectly live session a month after they
   signed in. Nothing had noticed because the first real sign-in was two days
   ago. `/api/me` now re-issues it, which costs no KV write.
2. **A failed renewal write was a 500 on every authenticated request.** The
   likeliest cause is the free tier's ~1,000 KV writes a day. The client reads a
   500 as "unknown", so the app would open and then do nothing. It is now
   caught.
3. **With those fixed, the pre-flight warning almost never fires.** That is
   correct: a checked session is ~30 days from its end. It exists for the
   degraded cases. So its driver step substitutes one phone's `/api/me` answer
   (the Worker half is unit-tested), and it is silent for dev tokens, which are
   always inside the 3-day window.
4. **`json()` in `worker/http.ts` spreads its headers**, so a `Headers` instance
   vanishes. The re-issued cookie was missing until a plain object was passed.
5. **Three routes, not one, lead to a stale field journal.** A journal records
   what *one* account acknowledged. Keep it across an account change and the new
   account's first sync deletes the old account's fields off the phone as
   "deleted elsewhere". Sign-out was the obvious route. The reviewer found the
   401 launch (a session that lapsed, or was ended in another tab) and a switch
   through "Sign in again" when Google's chooser returns a different account.
   All three now go through `forgetAccount`. A new route into a different
   account must too.
6. **`check-games` holds home to two screens with seven games**, and a separate
   account line broke it (1812 px against 1800). The account now sits in the
   title row (1771 px).
7. **Two `wrangler dev` processes started together collide on the inspector
   port** (9230), and the second one dies quietly. Pass `--inspector-port` when
   running two.
