## What was done

The owner challenged the field calibration model: four corners rather than two,
interpolating between the edges, inverted by iteration. **The challenge was right
about the corners and the measurement decided the rest.**

Simulated 400 calibrations against 8 m squares at 3 m of error per tap, asking the
only question that matters — standing on a square's centre, does the model name
that square?

| model | RMS error | wrong square |
|---|---|---|
| 2 taps, similarity (what was built) | 3.65 m | **22.3 %** |
| 4 taps, least-squares similarity | 2.65 m | 6.7 % |
| 4 taps, least-squares affine (**chosen**) | 3.02 m | **11.9 %** |
| 4 taps, bilinear interpolation | 3.19 m | 14.1 % |

So: four taps, walked round the perimeter (a1, h1, h8, a8), fitted as a
**least-squares affine** map. Boards may now be rectangles or parallelograms.
Decision 0028 records it, including why not the two rejected models.

- **`src/shared/field.ts`** — the fit, and the split of board space into
  `BoardPoint` (metric, rigid frame) and `BoardIndex` (which-square, affine
  inverse). `distanceFromBoardPointToSquareM` became point-to-parallelogram.
- **`views/calibrate.ts`** — four steps round the edge, with copy naming the
  piece on each corner; re-tap any of them and land back on the review.
- **`render.ts`** — squares are filled paths, not `fillRect`.
- **`fieldlink.ts`** — format 2 carries four corners; format 1 is still written
  whenever the board is square, so a square field's QR did not grow.
- **`scripts/check-calibrate.mjs`** — the eighth driver, and the only one that
  walks calibration through the UI rather than seeding a field.
- 16 new tests. **569 pass**, up from 553.

## What was learned

**Two taps were worst because of the count, not the shape.** The intuition to
check was "does the model match the ground"; the bigger effect was "how many
measurements does the fit get". Two points and four unknowns is exactly
determined — the fit passes through both whatever they are. That is also why a
mis-tap used to be *mathematically invisible*, which is the second thing four
corners buy.

**An interpolation through the taps is the one thing to avoid.** Bilinear passes
exactly through four noisy points, so GPS error at a corner becomes permanent
distortion in the board's shape. It came third on square ground *and* third on the
rectangular ground it was meant to be better at. Its extra freedom over affine can
only describe a non-parallelogram, and a chessboard on flat ground always is one.

**Affine cost accuracy against similarity, knowingly.** Least-squares similarity
was the most accurate model tested (6.7%), but on a 10 x 6 pitch it misidentifies
three quarters of the squares *with no noise at all*, because it is describing a
different shape. Affine gets that exactly right. Recorded as an explicit trade in
0028 rather than buried.

**Residual checking is weaker than it sounds, and I had the reason wrong at
first.** I claimed a corner dragged along an axis would be absorbed as shear, with
zero residual. It is not: moving *one* corner is never a parallelogram. Moving
*two* is, and that really does come back zero. A single corner out by `d` shows up
at about `d/4`, which set the warning threshold. Both cases are now tests.

**Splitting `BoardPoint` into two types was what made the refactor safe.** Deleting
`geo.squareM` rather than redefining it turned every affected site into a compile
error — twenty-four of them across nine files, none silent. A `squareM` that
quietly became a mean would have left reach and rendering subtly wrong on exactly
the boards this change exists to support.

**A driver bug wearing a product bug's clothes, and it is O-07's shape again.**
`check-scan.mjs` began failing "the camera is stopped on the way out". The scan
screen has its own `[data-join]` button, so `waitForSelector('[data-join]')`
matched the screen it was trying to leave and read the camera state before the
teardown ran. Fixed by waiting for `[data-video]` to be *detached* — which is the
precondition the claim actually rests on. It was a latent flake that predates this
work; the calibration change only changed the timing enough to expose it.
