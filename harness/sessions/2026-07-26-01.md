## What was learned

**Decision 0001's claims about the special moves were unverified assertions, and
they hold.** Castling really is driven entirely by `e1-g1` with the rook never
named; en passant really does only visit the capturing pawn's two squares while
the pawn on f5 disappears. Worth having checked — those were the two places the
reach rules had the most room to be quietly wrong.

**A flaky test found a real harness bug, and the symptom blamed the wrong thing.**
The full-game test failed roughly one run in four with
`implausible: 34 m in 0.0 s` — which reads as a rules bug. It was not. `Client.next()`
searches *already-received* messages, and both players see every broadcast, so
"wait until a carry exists" matched a state left over from the **opponent's**
carry. `walked()` then backdated a row that did not exist yet, and the place
landed with zero elapsed time. The predicate now matches the specific lift and
the buffer is cleared first. Six consecutive clean runs before committing.
**Lesson: a predicate over a shared broadcast stream must identify itself.**

**The analyser's first verdict was wrong-headed, and a scary wrong answer would
have been worse than none.** It originally judged "can the phone tell which
square I am on", concluded 8 m squares were unusable at σ=3, and recommended
19 m squares — a 154 m board. But the game's rule is *reach*, and reach grows
with reported accuracy, so it partly compensates for its own noise. Rewritten to
measure the refusal rate for a legitimate move, the same trace says 8 m squares
play fine with 0% refusals, while the *displayed* square is wrong 38% of the
time. Those have completely different fixes: one is a rendering problem, the
other would be a broken game.

**A cross-check worth recording.** The analyser, running on synthetic traces,
independently reproduced the phantom-distance figure the simulator predicted for
decision 0020 — 17.6 km/hour against 19 — from entirely separate code.

**Pruning by a client timestamp is a trap.** `SurveyDO` first expired traces by
the phone's `startedAt`, so a device with a wrong clock had its trace deleted the
instant it uploaded — precisely the phone whose data is most worth having. Now
the server clock, matching the rule the game already applies to move timing.
