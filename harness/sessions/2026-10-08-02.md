### The review

**Round 1, CLEAN**, with one correction to the implementer's reasoning.
Handlers **do** await the UserDO partway through a message: `syncIndex` and
`pushRecord` in `startIfBothReady` (from `pos` or `ready`), `onPause` and
`finish`. An await on anything but storage lets the next message start. The
reviewer proved this with a 300 ms timer. Today those calls come at the end,
after everything the tests read, so every current use of `handled` is
sound. The caveat is now in the comment on `handled`, under 10.13.1 and in
`reference/gotchas.md`. The coordinator also asked for the 2 s timeout and
for the preview sweep.

## What was learned

- **"Wait for the next `state`" is not a barrier on this socket.** The
  connect snapshot and the broadcasts of a lift or a place are unasked-for
  `state`s, and a `sync` reply can be confused with any of them.
- **An unknown message type is a free, exact barrier**, because the error
  echoes the type. It costs one inbound message in a test, and none in
  production.
- **A Durable Object's input gate opens on any await that is not its own
  storage.** Messages from one socket are then no longer handled strictly
  one after another. That is the limit of `handled`, and it matters for any
  future handler that calls another object before writing what a test reads.
- **`expect` is slow in a loop.** About 4 µs a call. A property sweep of
  hundreds of thousands of cases should collect failures and assert once.
- `sockets.map(handled)` would pass the index as the timeout. `settle` wraps
  the call.
