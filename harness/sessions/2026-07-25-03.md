## What was learned

**The validation order was wrong, and the symptom was a lie.** `checkCarry` ran
before chess legality, so a player who tried an illegal move quickly enough was
told *"your GPS jumped"* — the carry check saw 24 m covered in no time and
returned `implausible`. Legality does not depend on where anyone is standing, so
it is both the cheaper check and the honest one. **Legality first.** This is the
kind of thing that would have been maddening to diagnose from a field.

**Three of the eight failures were the tests being wrong about the world**, which
is worth more than the bug:

1. Lift and place arrived in the same millisecond, so a legitimate 8 m walk read
   as a teleport. The guard was right to fire — a real walk takes seconds. Rather
   than weaken a genuine anti-cheat rule or sleep whole seconds in every test, a
   `walked()` helper backdates the pending lift via SQL.
2. A test asserted black's clock was *exactly* its starting value after white
   moved. It was 12 ms short, because black's clock is **running** by then. The
   test had assumed a frozen clock.
3. The mate test's position was not mate. `Qxf7` with no bishop on c4 is a
   blunder — the king just takes the queen. Replaced with the real Scholar's mate
   position.

**Losing context is not hypothetical, and stale harness files cost real time.**
Mid-session my context was compacted across the phase 3 work. `STATE.md` still
said "active stage 1.9", so on waking I read my own uncommitted `game-do.ts`

go, not at the end** — it is the only thing standing between a compacted context
and a wrong conclusion about who did what.
