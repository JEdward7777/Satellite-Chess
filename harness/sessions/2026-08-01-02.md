## What was learned

**Silence means standing still, not staleness.** `OppPosMsg.at` was commented
"so the client can age out a stale dot", and that instinct is wrong here. The
send policy relays only on movement, so a player standing on their back rank
sends **one** message and then nothing for the rest of the game. Age out on
silence and the most common state in the game — someone waiting for you to walk
— renders as an opponent who has left. The honest signal is `connected`, which
the snapshot already carries, so the dot is solid or hollow and never faded by a
timer. The comment on `at` has been corrected.

**The same fact makes the snapshot copy necessary rather than merely tidy.**
A relay-only dot means a client that connects, reconnects, or joins late has
nothing to draw until the other player happens to walk two metres. For the
staging phase — both players standing still, waiting — that is never. So the
snapshot repeats each player's last relayed position. Outbound is not billed, so
this is free; it is the same reasoning that made full snapshots cheaper than a
delta protocol in the first place.

**Interpolate backwards, not forwards.** The dot lags by about one relay
interval: it is always on its way to where they already are. Extrapolating along
their last heading would put it ahead of them and snatch it back the moment they
turned. For atmosphere, a smooth lie about two seconds ago beats a jittery guess
about now — and nothing depends on this number, so latency is the cheap thing to
spend.

**Two clamps stop it looking broken**, and both are cases the naive version gets
wrong in a way that reads as a bug in something else:
- The glide is capped at `POS_MIN_INTERVAL_MS`. Otherwise a player who stood
  still for two minutes and then took one step is animated creeping for two
  minutes.
- A fix that could not have been *walked* to (>10 m/s) snaps instead of gliding.
  An urban-canyon GPS throw is a glitch, and animating it smoothly is a lie
  about a walk that never happened.

**The glide starts from where the dot is, not from the last fix.** Otherwise an
early relay teleports it backwards before moving it forwards. One line, and it
is the difference between "smooth" and "twitchy".

**Repainting has to be gated on motion.** The screen is held on all game
(`wakelock.ts`) on a phone outdoors that is probably not on charge, so a 10 Hz
canvas redraw for a whole game is a real cost — and buys nothing, because for
most of a game nothing is moving. `OpponentDot.moving` exists for exactly this:
the interval starts when a glide starts and stops when it lands.

**The browser check was worth it again, and it is now cheap.** `drive-game.mjs`
grew two steps and a `opponentDot()` helper that finds the dot by its colour in
the canvas and returns its centre. That turns "does it glide?" into a number:
sampled once a second while black walked, y went 51, 51, 58, 64, 68, 78, 88, 93 —
continuous, not the 0,0,jump pattern a raw relay would give. After black's
context closed, the same reading dropped from ~98 coloured pixels to 25: the
hollow ring. A screenshot alone could not have told either of those apart.
