## What was done

Folded the field walk's numbers back into the rules — but the operator reframed
the problem first, and the reframing is the substance of the session.

**The correction.** I opened by offering, per session `2026-09-06-03`, to tune
the *square size* from the trace. The operator pointed out that square size is
not an independent variable: a field is whatever ground people have, the board is
eight squares along it, so `squareM = fieldLength / 8` is an input. You cannot
answer a GPS problem by asking for a bigger field — the players end up in the
bushes. The independent variable is **reach**, which has two failure modes
pulling opposite ways: too large and you play ordinary chess standing in the
middle of the field, too small and you fight the lock while your clock runs. It
should be a parameter set before the game, in fractional squares.

That directly reverses the second half of decision 0023 ("when accuracy turns out
worse, the answer is bigger squares — and therefore a bigger field"). Written up
as **decision 0031**; 0023 carries a `Superseded by` line naming which half fell.

**What the trace then said.** Pulled the raw trace off the live SurveyDO
(`/api/survey/traces` — note the plural, I wasted a call on `/trace`) and
measured against it directly rather than through `analyse-survey.mjs`, because
the questions were new. Scripts are in the scratchpad, not committed; the numbers
that mattered are quoted in 0031.

**The code.** `ReachConfig` now carries `baseSquares` / `minSquares` /
`maxSquares` and no absolute distance in metres; metres are derived from
`FieldGeometry.meanSquareM` at the point of use. `effectiveReachM` gained a
`squareM` parameter, which made the compiler enumerate every call site — there
were only four outside `reach.ts`. The handicap moved to squares too
(`reachBonusM` → `reachBonusSquares` on the wire, `white_reach_bonus_sq` in
SQLite, schema 2 with an idempotent `ALTER TABLE` upgrade in `applySchema`).
`baseSquares` is a create-screen dial stepping 0.05 squares, and the game's reach
rule is now snapshotted per game in `reach_json` — previously the DO *sent*
`reach` in every snapshot but always validated against `DEFAULT_REACH`, so a
per-game rule would have been advisory only.

## What was learned

**The game was already degenerate, and nobody had noticed.** Reach was
`baseM 5 + reportedAccuracy`. The walked device never reported better than
**3.00 m** and its median was **3.37 m**, so ordinary play had **8.4 m of reach
on 8 m squares — 1.05 squares, with no handicap set at all**. You could lift a
piece from a square you were not standing on. `MAX_HANDICAP_M = 4` from stage
6.1.1 was holding a door shut in a wall that was already down. This is the thing
the walk actually paid for, and it is not what session `2026-09-06-03` concluded.

**Reported accuracy is not a measurement at this scale.** Median 3.37 m claimed
against 0.21 m actual over the three-minute static hold — ~16x pessimistic, with
3.00 m looking like a reporting floor rather than an observation. Hence the
`goodAccuracyM` model: accuracy at or below an ordinary fix costs nothing, and
only genuine excess widens the circle. 0023's graceful-degradation argument
survives unchanged above that threshold.

**Session `2026-09-06-03`'s headline recommendation was wrong, twice over.** It
proposed averaging several fixes per corner to fix its 5.8 m repeatability
finding.

1. *Averaging does nothing.* The A1→A2 gap is 5.76 m from a single fix, 5.73 m
   averaged over 10 s, 5.42 m over 30 s, and worse at 60 s (the window starts
   eating the walk). Within-window scatter is already 0.02–0.15 m — there is
   nothing to average away. Averaging attacks scatter; scatter was never the
   problem. Bias wanders over minutes, and a few seconds of averaging cannot
   touch it.
2. *It probably was not the receiver.* Same spot re-measured with no walk in
   between (`settle`→`static`, 154 s apart) moved **0.48 m**. And the legs from A
   out to B and C agree across both A readings to 0.15 m and 0.01 m, where a
   genuine 5.8 m frame shift predicts 1.39 m and 0.57 m. Most likely the operator
   restood ~5.8 m away — a *human* calibration error.
   **Caveat, and it is a real one:** B and C were only 8° apart in bearing and
   both ~80° off the shift direction, which is close to the geometry that blinds
   this test. It leans strongly, it does not prove. Tapping one corner twice from
   a physical mark would settle it in thirty seconds on the next trip.

**The distance accumulator was left alone, deliberately.** The plan said to
consider relaxing the anti-drift constants, and the trace supports it: replaying
`DistanceAccumulator` over the real fixes credits 84.0 m of a 104 m walk at
`ANCHOR_ACCURACY_FACTOR = 2` and 92.8 m at 1, with **zero** phantom distance from
either during four minutes of standing still. I made the change — and reverted
it, because `test/gps.test.ts` immediately failed with 1525 m of phantom distance
per hour. Those tests model a phone whose true error really is as large as it
claims, which is the honest assumption; my trace only proves *this* handset is
good. Distance is the currency of the whole game (decision 0019), so over-counting
is much worse than losing 9% of a walk. Logged as **O-12** with the real fix
named: scale the floor by observed scatter rather than claimed accuracy.
