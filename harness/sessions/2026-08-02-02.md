## What was done

`5.3.4`, player-requested pause, and **O-04** — nothing decided what happened to a
game whose opponent never returned — settled together, because a deliberate pause
and an abandonment land in exactly the same frozen state and two rules for one
condition would be two ways out of it. **Decision 0025** records the rule and the
argument.

The rule, as the owner set it: a suspended game freezes indefinitely and is never
deleted on a timer; after **30 days** the other player may claim the win by a
button that is never automatic and never expires; the opponent may still resume
right up until it is pressed; and clearing out old games becomes something the app
*offers* rather than something it *does*.

- **`src/worker/game-do.ts`** — `onPause` banks the clock, sets `suspended`,
  cancels the flag alarm and puts a carried piece back (decision 0009): the same
  path `suspendForDisconnect` takes, deliberately, so there is one frozen state.
  `suspensionFor` computes each player's view of it, `onClaim` enforces
  eligibility, and `finish` now *cancels* the `gc` timer instead of scheduling a
  deletion.
- **`src/worker/schema.ts`** — `suspended_at`, `suspended_by`, with a comment
  explaining why the responsible player has to be recorded at all.
- **`src/shared/protocol.ts`** — `ClaimMsg`, `CLAIM_AFTER_MS`, `cannot_claim`, and
  `GameSnapshot.suspension`. `FINISHED_GAME_TTL_MS` and `ABANDONED_GAME_TTL_MS`
  deleted.
- **`src/client/views/game.ts`** — `[data-pause]`, `[data-claim]` behind a
  confirmation, and `suspendedPrompt`, which says three different things depending
  on who is reading it.
- 14 new tests (9 in `test/worker/carry.test.ts`, 5 in `test/game.test.ts`). **476
  pass.** Verified in two browsers against a real `wrangler dev`: pause, the three
  messages, the claim button correctly hidden before the month is up, resume.

## What was learned

**"Claim if your opponent is offline" inverts the rule, and it is the obvious
implementation.** After thirty days *nobody* is connected, so the player who
walked off could open the app and claim the win against the person who stayed. The
fix is to record who caused the suspension and exclude them — one column, and the
whole rule turns on it. Written into `STATE.md` and into the schema comment,
because it will look like a redundancy to anyone simplifying the code later.

**A dead constant contradicted a live decision.** `ABANDONED_GAME_TTL_MS` implied
deleting abandoned games after 14 days — a fortnight *before* the claim button
would have appeared. It was never wired to anything, which is exactly why it
survived: an unused constant reads as harmless while it quietly encodes a rule
nobody agreed to. Deleted rather than adjusted.

**The incentive argument is what settles ties in game rules.** Draw-on-abandon and
void-on-abandon both sound kinder, and both make closing the app the correct move
in a lost position. The rule does not have to be perfectly fair; it has to be
worse than losing honestly. That framing decided a question that had been open
since 2026-07-25.

**A month-long suspension makes the game index load-bearing.** Until `2.3.4`
exists, a paused game's join code is the only handle on it — so a game can now
outlive the player's memory of how to reach it. Two stages added under it:
`2.3.4.1`, list suspended games with the claim countdown; `2.3.4.2`, the prompt
that *offers* to clear old games, and must never be able to remove one that is
still claimable.
